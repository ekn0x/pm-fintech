# Verify: Transformed (2024) skill set

Built from my knowledge of *Transformed: Moving to the Product Operating Model* (Cagan with Hickman, Jones, Idiodi, Moore; Wiley, March 2024), not from the text. There is only one edition. For each skill: the claims I attribute to the book, my confidence, and where to check in your copy. Everything under "General practice" is mine or standard industry practice, not the book's. Part and chapter names are as I recall them; check the table of contents if one doesn't match.

Confidence: **High** = core, frequently restated concept. **Medium** = confident of the idea, less of the exact framing or list. **Low** = check before relying on it.

---

## transformed-model-core

| Claim | Confidence | Where to check |
|---|---|---|
| The book's subject: moving from the IT/project model to the product operating model | High | Opening chapters; Part II, "Transformation Defined" |
| Three dimensions of change: how you build, how you solve problems, how you decide which problems to solve | High | Part II, one chapter per dimension |
| IT model: technology serves "the business"; stakeholders as customers of the team; product model: serve customers in ways that work for the business | High | Part II |
| Five product model concepts: product teams, product strategy, product discovery, product delivery, product culture | High | "Product Model Concepts" part |
| Principles per concept (empowered/outcomes/ownership/collaboration; focus/insights/transparency/bets; minimize waste/assess risks/rapid experimentation/test responsibly; small-frequent-uncoupled releases/instrumentation/monitoring/deployment infrastructure; principles over process/trust over control/innovation over predictability/learning over failure) | Medium-High | "Product Model Principles" material. Check exact labels |
| Four competencies: PM, product designer, tech lead, product leaders | High | "Product Model Competencies" part |
| Agile/process-only transformations as a failure pattern | Medium-High | Part I/II, transformation anti-patterns |
| Case studies span B2C, B2B and non-tech enterprises (I recall Trainline, Datasite, Gympass, Kaiser Permanente, Almosafer) | Low-Medium | Case study chapters. Check names and count |

General practice: the comparison table wording, "renaming" as a named failure pattern, the routing table, the B2B/B2C/regulated notes.
